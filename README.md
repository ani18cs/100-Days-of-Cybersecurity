# 100 Days of Cybersecurity

## The "Why": The Need for This Challenge
As a third-year engineering student at DSATM, I am at a critical juncture where bridging the gap between academic theory and real-world application is paramount. While I have a strong foundation and a deep interest in domains like network forensics, VPN architectures (IKE/ESP), and the intersection of AI and security (like AI-driven phishing firewalls), consuming tutorials and lectures is no longer enough. 

To stand out for upcoming internships and roles in the cybersecurity industry, I need a verifiable track record of practical execution. The cybersecurity field demands proof of competence, relentless curiosity, and the ability to articulate technical findings. This 100-day challenge is my commitment to public accountability. By building, breaking, and analyzing systems consistently, I am transforming passive knowledge into documented, practical expertise.

## The "How": My Methodology
For the next 100 days, I am treating this repository as my public lab notebook. I will dedicate at least 1 to 1.5 hours daily to focused, hands-on cybersecurity work. 

My approach is structured around consistent progression and tangible outputs:

### 1. Hands-On Execution
Instead of just reading about security, I will deploy environments, analyze raw data, and write code. My daily focus will rotate through core pillars:
*   **Network & Traffic Analysis:** Capturing and dissecting packets, studying encapsulation, and understanding how data moves (and hides) on a network.
*   **Linux & Command-Line Mastery:** Building muscle memory for rapid navigation, file manipulation, and system administration.
*   **Custom Tooling:** Writing Python scripts to automate tasks, from basic port scanners to more complex data obfuscation and steganography tools.
*   **Vulnerability Research:** Completing CTFs, engaging with platforms like TryHackMe and OverTheWire, and documenting the exploit chains.

### 2. Meticulous Documentation
"If it isn't documented, it didn't happen." 
Every day, I will push an update to this repository. This won't just be raw code; it will include:
*   **Markdown Write-ups:** Step-by-step methodologies of labs completed or PCAP files analyzed.
*   **Code with Context:** Custom scripts uploaded with clear `README.md` files explaining the objective, tools used, and execution instructions.
*   **Daily Log:** A running chronological log at the bottom of this master README detailing exactly what I accomplished each day.

### 3. Building in Public
Learning in a vacuum limits growth. I will actively distill my daily or weekly technical learnings into concise insights and share them with the broader professional community to gather feedback, learn from industry experts, and refine my understanding.
